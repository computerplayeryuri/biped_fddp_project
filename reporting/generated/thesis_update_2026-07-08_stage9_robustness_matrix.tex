\section{Stage 9 Software Robustness Matrix}
\label{sec:stage9_software_robustness_matrix_20260708}

To strengthen the stability and robustness evidence, a Stage 9 software-only
robustness matrix was added around the current scripted C2 alternating-step
preview. The test matrix extends the previous short Stage 8 contact-transition
preview to longer durations and additional stress conditions: long-duration
operation, friction variation, slope-equivalent loading, small raised toe-step
contacts, and external push pulses.

The matrix should be interpreted carefully. It validates the current software
stack and MuJoCo contact-event plumbing under scripted periodic stepping. It is
not a newly solved full FDDP walking trajectory, and it is not hardware walking
validation. It is nevertheless useful because it turns the robustness claim
from an untested statement into a measured simulation envelope.

The most stable part of the envelope is long-duration scripted stepping on
flat terrain. The \SI{60}{\second} case matched 119/120 scheduled contact
events, kept peak pitch below \SI{0.81}{\degree}, and did not trigger a fall.
Friction variation from $\mu=0.50$ to $\mu=1.10$ and slope-equivalent
loading up to $\pm\SI{6}{\degree}$ also passed the current acceptance criteria.

The matrix also identifies the current robustness boundary. A
\SI{2}{\milli\metre} raised toe step passed, but \SI{4}{\milli\metre} and
\SI{6}{\milli\metre} steps failed the event-matching/contact robustness
criteria. Push pulses of $\pm\SI{2}{\newton}$ passed, while $\pm\SI{4}{\newton}$
caused transient support contact loss without a fall, and $\pm\SI{6}{\newton}$
crossed the \SI{30}{\degree} pitch fall threshold after approximately
\SI{2.2}{\second}. These failures should be reported as measured limits of the
current scripted controller rather than hidden or relaxed.

The resulting conclusion is therefore:

\begin{quote}
The current software stack is stable under the tested long-duration, friction,
slope-equivalent, low-step-height, and small-push simulation conditions. The
current scripted controller is not robust to larger toe-step perturbations or
large push pulses. Full FDDP walking-gait validation and hardware walking
experiments remain required before claiming general robust biped locomotion.
\end{quote}
